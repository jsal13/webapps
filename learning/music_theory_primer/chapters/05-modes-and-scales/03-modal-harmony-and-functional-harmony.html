Chapter 5, Section 3

Modal Harmony & Functional Harmony

Modal harmony and functional harmony are not opposites so much as two different ways of organizing musical gravity: one emphasizes a scale's identity, the other emphasizes a tonic-centered system of tension and release.

Modal harmony

In modal music, the collection itself may carry more weight than the specific tonic-dominant relationship. A mode can sound stable without relying on a classic V-I cadence, because the scale's tonal center and interval pattern are already built into the material.

Modal harmony often exists in music where a single mode governs the piece, and the listener accepts the central pitch as an axis rather than as the destination of a functional progression.

Functional harmony

Functional harmony, typical in much tonal music, treats the tonic, dominant, and subdominant as distinct harmonic functions. The dominant pulls toward the tonic, and the progression within a key creates clear expectations that can be delayed, deferred, or altered.

These systems can coexist. A piece may use modal color within a functional framework, or it may adopt a more scale-driven harmonic identity for a section while still preserving tonal goals elsewhere.

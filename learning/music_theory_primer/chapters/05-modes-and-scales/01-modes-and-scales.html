Chapter 5, Section 1

Diatonic Modes

The diatonic modes are seven scale collections generated from the same major-key pitch set. They differ in which degree is treated as the tonal center, creating different colors and tendencies.

Seven modal colors

Starting from a major scale, each degree can serve as a mode: Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian, and the parent major mode itself. The most commonly discussed in tonal practice are Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, and Locrian.

Each mode has a characteristic mood. Dorian feels minor with a raised sixth, Phrygian carries a dark, flat-second color, Lydian has a raised fourth that brightens the scale, Mixolydian contains a lowered seventh, and Locrian is the most unstable and tense of the set.

Why modes matter

Modes do not merely replace one key signature with another; they shift the hierarchy of pitch classes. The notes may be the same, but their gravitational pull changes according to which degree is heard as the center.

When learning modes, listen for the scale degree that behaves like a tonal center and also note the most active dissonant tones. These two factors define each mode's personality.

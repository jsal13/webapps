Four-Part SATB Writing

SATB writing models a four-voice texture: soprano, alto, tenor, and bass. Each line should be singable on its own while the four lines combine into clear harmony.

The four voices

The soprano is the highest voice and often carries the melody. The alto fills the upper-middle register, the tenor occupies the lower-middle register, and the bass supplies the harmonic foundation. Keep the parts in a comfortable range for the singers you are writing for.

Spacing and doubling

Keep adjacent upper voices reasonably close: no more than an octave between soprano and alto or between alto and tenor. The bass may sit farther below the tenor. In a root-position triad, double the root as a reliable default.

Example: For I in C major, a practical voicing is bass C2, tenor G3, alto E4, soprano C5. The chord is complete, the root is doubled, and the upper voices stay within an octave.

Chord completeness

In four voices, triads usually contain all three chord members, with one member doubled. Seventh chords normally contain all four members and are not doubled. Avoid crossing voices: the alto should not rise above the soprano, and the tenor should not rise above the alto.

A useful priority order is \(\text{correct harmony} \rightarrow \text{singable lines} \rightarrow \text{good spacing}\).

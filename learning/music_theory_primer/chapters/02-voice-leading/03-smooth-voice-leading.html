Smooth Voice Leading

Good voice leading makes each part feel like a coherent melody. The best motion is often the smallest motion that satisfies the harmonic change.

Keep common tones

When two neighboring chords share a pitch, let one voice keep it when practical. This gives the ear a stable thread while the other voices move.

Example: In C major, I (C-E-G) to vi (A-C-E) shares C and E. Keep C in the soprano and E in the alto; move the other voices by step when possible.

Prefer stepwise motion

Move voices by whole or half step when you can. Small intervals are easier to sing and make the direction of each line clear. Leaps are useful for variety, but balance a large leap with movement in the opposite direction.

Use contrary and oblique motion

Contrary motion sends voices in opposite directions and helps preserve independence. Oblique motion holds one voice while another moves, making it especially effective for retaining common tones.

A useful melodic balance is \(\text{leap upward} \rightarrow \text{stepwise motion downward}\), or the reverse.

Voice leading is a hierarchy of decisions: preserve essential chord tones, resolve tendency tones, avoid prohibited parallels in the style, and then choose the most singable line.

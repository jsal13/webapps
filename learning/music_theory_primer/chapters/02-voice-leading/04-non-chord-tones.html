Chapter 2, Section 4

Non-Chord Tones

A non-chord tone is a note that decorates or connects the harmony without belonging to the chord sounding beneath it. Its approach, placement, and resolution give it its name.

Passing and neighbor tones

A passing tone fills a stepwise gap between two chord tones: C-D-E, where D is the passing tone. A neighbor tone leaves a chord tone by step and returns to it: E-F-E.

Suspensions and appoggiaturas

A suspension is prepared as a chord tone, held into the next harmony, and then resolved downward by step. In a G7-C context, an F held over the C chord can resolve down to E.

An appoggiatura is an accented, approached non-chord tone that resolves by step, often from a leap. It creates expressive dissonance on a strong beat rather than simply filling a scale.

Pedal points

A pedal point is a sustained or repeated pitch, usually in the bass, that begins as a chord tone, becomes dissonant as harmonies change, and returns to consonance. A dominant pedal can build tension before a final tonic.

Example: Hold or repeat G in the bass while the upper voices move through ii6, V, and I. G is consonant in V, dissonant against ii6, and consonant again when the dominant function returns.

The basic suspension pattern is \(\text{preparation} \rightarrow \text{dissonance} \rightarrow \text{stepwise resolution}\).

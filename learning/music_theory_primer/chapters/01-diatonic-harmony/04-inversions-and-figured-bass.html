Chord Inversions and Figured Bass

An inversion changes which chord member is in the bass while preserving the chord's identity. This gives the bass line smoother motion and creates different spacing choices above it.

Triad positions

A root-position triad has the root in the bass and is written with no figure. A first inversion has the third in the bass and is marked 6; a second inversion has the fifth in the bass and is marked 6/4.

Example: C-E-G is C in root position; E-G-C is C6; G-C-E is C6/4. All three contain the same three pitch classes.

Seventh-chord positions

Seventh chords use 7 in root position, 6/5 in first inversion, 4/3 in second inversion, and 4/2 (or 2) in third inversion.

Figured bass in practice

Figured bass gives the interval recipe above a written bass note. From a bass C, the figure 6 asks for notes a sixth and a third above C. Performers fill in the exact chord tones according to the key and context.

For a triad: \(\text{root position} = 5/3\), \(\text{first inversion} = 6/3\), \(\text{second inversion} = 6/4\).

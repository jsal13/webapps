Roman Numeral Analysis

Roman numerals describe a chord by its scale-degree root and quality, letting musicians analyze a progression independently of key.

Reading the symbols

In a major key, the basic diatonic set is I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°. Uppercase numerals identify major triads, lowercase numerals identify minor triads, and vii° identifies a diminished triad.

In C major, the progression C-Am-Dm-G is analyzed as I-vi-ii-V. Transpose it to G major and the pitches change, but the analysis remains I-vi-ii-V.

Adding detail

Arabic numbers after a numeral identify a seventh chord or an inversion. For example, V7 means a dominant seventh chord, while ii6 means a first-inversion ii chord.

A useful summary is: \(\text{Roman numeral} = \text{scale-degree root} + \text{chord quality}\).

Example: In G major, the notes A-C-E form ii; the same chord shape in C major is D-F-A, also ii.

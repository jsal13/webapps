Diatonic Function

Function describes what a chord does in a key: where it feels at rest, where it moves away, and where it creates pressure to return.

Three functional families

Tonic is the home area. It includes I and often vi, which can prolong or substitute for tonic.

Subdominant, also called predominant, moves away from tonic. The main example is ii; IV serves the same broad role.

Dominant creates forward pressure toward tonic. V and vii° contain scale degree 7, the leading tone, which pulls upward to scale degree 1.

A functional sentence

Example: In C major, Dm7-G7-C is ii7-V7-I: predominant, dominant, then tonic.

This is not a rigid rulebook. A progression can delay a resolution, substitute a chord, or stay in one function for several measures. Functional labels are a way to hear and describe the larger motion.

The common direction is \(\text{tonic} \rightarrow \text{predominant} \rightarrow \text{dominant} \rightarrow \text{tonic}\).

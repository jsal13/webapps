Chapter 8, Section 2

Invertible Counterpoint & Fugue

In counterpoint, inversion means that the musical lines may exchange registers while preserving the intervallic logic of the texture.

Invertible counterpoint

Invertible counterpoint is designed so that the voices can be exchanged without breaking the musical logic. This is especially useful in imitative and stretto passages, where the same material may reappear at a different interval.

Composers often use invertible counterpoint to maintain balance and smoothness while allowing tighter structural development.

Fugue

A fugue is a contrapuntal form built around a subject, which is then answered by another voice. The answer may be exact or tonal. The subject can be accompanied by a countersubject, while episodes link statements of the subject and develop motives in between.

Stretto occurs when the subject enters before the previous statement has fully resolved, creating a more intense, compressed texture.

Chapter 8, Section 3

Canon & Imitation

A canon or imitation is a musical idea repeated in another voice, either at the same or a transformed interval. The texture stays coherent because the new entrance preserves recognizable material.

Canon

A canon is a strict imitation in which one voice follows another after a fixed interval and time delay. The interval may be at the unison, octave, fifth, or another consonant interval, depending on the compositional goal.

Imitation

Imitation is broader than canon: the same or similar subject can appear in another voice with varying intervals, rhythmic alterations, or development. It is often used to create continuity without enforcing the strictness of canon.

These devices are central to tonal and contrapuntal composition because they make musical identity travel through the texture while preserving coherence and clarity.

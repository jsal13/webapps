Chapter 10, Section 3

Functional Ear Training

Ear training is not just pitch recognition; it is the ability to hear function, expectation, and tonal movement as music unfolds.

Recognizing harmonic function

A listener can learn to hear whether a sonority behaves like tonic, dominant, or pre-dominant function. This often begins with recognizing chord quality and its tendency to resolve.

As experience grows, the ear learns to detect progression patterns, cadences, and the brief tonicizations or modulations that create harmonic momentum.

Practical approach

Listen first for the overall tonal center, then for the voice leading and cadence, then for the larger phrase. Functional ear training becomes more reliable when you connect what you hear to how a progression is expected to move.

In other words, good ear training is not passive memorization; it is the hearing of musical expectation and its fulfillment.

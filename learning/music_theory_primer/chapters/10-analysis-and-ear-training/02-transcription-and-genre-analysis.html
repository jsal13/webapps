Chapter 10, Section 2

Transcription & Genre Analysis

The best way to understand musical style is to listen closely and then write down what you hear. Transcription turns perception into evidence.

Transcription

Transcribing music strengthens listening by forcing the analyst to decide on pitch, rhythm, form, and articulation. It is especially useful when learning to hear a musical language outside the classroom context.

Even a short passage can reveal a lot about rhythmic asymmetry, melodic contour, harmonic rhythm, and phrase structure.

Genre analysis

Different genres privilege different musical features. Pop music may foreground harmonic rhythm and repeated forms; jazz may foreground improvisation, substitutions, and timbral color; classical music may foreground thematic transformation and formal planning; and folk or world music may foreground modal inflection and rhythmic cycle.

Analyzing one’s own genre is powerful because it connects theory to the music students actually hear, play, and love.

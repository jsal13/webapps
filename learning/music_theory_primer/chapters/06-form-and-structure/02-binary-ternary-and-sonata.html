Binary, Ternary, and Sonata Form

Large forms organize repeated and contrasting sections into a coherent shape. Binary, ternary, and sonata form each balance contrast with return in a different way.

Binary form

Binary form generally divides into two sections: A and B. The second section often provides contrast in key, texture, or harmonic motion. In rounded binary form, material from A returns at the end of B.

Ternary form

Ternary form is typically A-B-A. The return of A helps the form feel balanced and complete, and the B section often sharpens contrast by changing key or texture.

Sonata form

Sonata form usually contains exposition, development, and recapitulation. The exposition introduces contrasting themes and often brings them into opposing keys; the development explores them; and the recapitulation restates them in a stable, often tonic-oriented solution.

Sonata form is especially powerful because it creates a dramatic sense of tension and resolution through tonal and thematic transformation.

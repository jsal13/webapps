Chapter 6, Section 1

Phrase Structure & Form

Form is the way musical ideas are organized over time. Even a short phrase can contain a motive, a cadence, and a clear sense of arrival or continuation.

Phrase-level units

A motive is the smallest identifiable musical idea. A phrase is a complete musical thought, often shaped by a cadence. A period usually consists of two phrases, with the second phrase answering or extending the first. A sentence typically presents a musical idea and then repeats or expands it quickly for closure.

These units matter because hearing musical structure is a matter of expectation and fulfillment. A phrase need not be long to feel complete; it simply needs enough material and closure to satisfy the listener.

Form beyond the phrase

At larger levels, composers organize phrases into sections that may repeat, contrast, or develop. The same rhythmic and melodic logic that makes a phrase coherent also helps a whole movement feel purposeful.

Worked example

Example: A short sentence often begins with a compressed motive, expands it, and then arrives on a cadence. In a basic tonic-dominant pattern, a phrase may move by step, then leap and return, before closing with a stable arrival.

Practice

Chromatic Mediants & Tonicization

Chromatic mediants are chords related by a mediant relationship but not limited to the diatonic collection, while tonicization adds a brief emphasis of a new center without fully establishing it.

Chromatic mediants

A chromatic mediant is often a chord that shares a common tone or a third-relationship with the tonic while using altered pitches. This creates a strong sense of harmonic color without necessarily changing the underlying key.

These progressions are especially common when composers want a surprising but still coherent shift in color. The novelty comes from altered pitch class content and from the re-interpretation of a familiar chord function.

Tonicization vs. modulation

Tonicization is a local emphasis of a new tonal center, usually through dominant function. A chord like V/ii or V/vi may briefly point toward ii or vi, but the music quickly returns to the original tonic. It feels like a detour, not a destination.

Modulation, by contrast, establishes the new key enough to persist. A cadence, phrase structure, or repeated harmonic pattern often confirms the destination. In tonal music, the distinction is often one of duration and confirmation rather than of harmonic content alone.

Neapolitan & Augmented Sixth Chords

These chromatic sonorities are notable for their strong tendency to resolve and for the way they sharpen tonal expectation without requiring a fully new key center.

Neapolitan sixth

A Neapolitan sixth is a major triad built on the lowered second scale degree. In C major this is D♭-F-A♭, a chromatic alteration of the supertonic area. Because it often appears in first inversion, it is called a sixth chord and is usually heard as a pre-dominant sonority.

The Neapolitan sixth typically resolves to V or to a dominant-function chord. It creates a strong contrast with the surrounding tonic and is often associated with dramatic or expressive passages.

Augmented sixth chords

Augmented sixth chords are named for the augmented sixth interval between their two most salient tones. They usually include a chromatic upper- or lower-neighbor tone to the dominant. The Italian, French, and German sixths are common categories, each distinguished by a slightly different collection of pitches.

These chords are especially effective before V because they intensify the dominant's pull. Their voice-leading is highly idiomatic: the augmented sixth resolves by moving to scale degree 5 while the other tones resolve smoothly to the dominant and tonic.

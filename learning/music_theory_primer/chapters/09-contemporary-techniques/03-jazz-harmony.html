Chapter 9, Section 3

Jazz Harmony

Jazz harmony expands functional harmony with extensions, alterations, reharmonization, and coloristic voice-leading that can be heard as part of a broader tonal vocabulary.

Extensions and alterations

Jazz chords often add sevenths, ninths, elevenths, and thirteenths, creating a richer harmonic spectrum. Altered tensions such as ♯9 or ♭13 can intensify dominant function and color the sound of the chord without necessarily abandoning its tonal role.

These extensions and alterations are often understood in relation to a scale or melodic collection, which is why chord-scale relationships are so important.

Reharmonization and voice-leading

Jazz harmony frequently treats a song as a framework for reharmonization, where the melody and groove remain but the underlying harmonic progression is rearranged. The goal can be motion, color, or a stronger connection to a particular style or improvisational practice.

Understanding jazz harmony means hearing how a chord functions as a momentary center, voice-leading structure, or color field rather than only as a triadic tonic-dominant relationship.

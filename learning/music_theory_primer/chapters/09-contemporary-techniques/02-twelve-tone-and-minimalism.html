Chapter 9, Section 2

Twelve-Tone, Serialism, and Minimalism

Modern music often replaces the tonal hierarchy with a pitch organization that is serial, cyclical, or process-driven.

Twelve-tone technique

Twelve-tone technique organizes the twelve chromatic pitch classes into a row, or series. The row may be used in its prime, inversion, retrograde, or retrograde inversion forms, creating a system of controlled variation without tonal hierarchy.

Serialism extends this logic beyond pitch alone, applying the same principle to rhythms, dynamics, or timbre in more fully organized compositions.

Minimalism

Minimalism does not reject tradition so much as postpone or suspend it. Repeated cells, breath-like pacing, and gradual transformation create musical motion without dramatic tonal or formal contrast. Process music often emphasizes transformation over statement and response.

These techniques may sound very different from tonal music, yet they are still based on organizing principles that listeners can learn to perceive.
